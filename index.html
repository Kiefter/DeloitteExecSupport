Prompting

How to get the most out of Copilot

A great prompt can have up to four parts — Goal, Context, Source, and Expectations. Only the goal is required; add the others to sharpen the result. The more direction you give, the more useful the answer.

1 The four building blocks

Goal · required

What you want Copilot to do. Start with a verb — "Summarize…", "Draft…", "Create a table…"

Context

Why you're asking and who it's for — "…for my executive ahead of the Business Operations Review."

Source

What Copilot should use. Point it at emails, a meeting, or a file by typing / — "…based on the Atlas launch emails this week."

Expectations

The shape of the answer — format, length, tone — "…in five bullets, friendly tone, under 150 words."

2 Put it together

Start with just a goal, then layer in the other parts to go from a generic answer to exactly what you need.

Just a goal → "Summarize my emails."
Goal + Context + Source + Expectations
Summarize the emails from the Atlas launch team over the past week so I can brief my executive ahead of tomorrow's Business Operations Review. Group them into what needs a decision and what's just an update, and keep it to five short bullet points.
Why it works: it names the goal (summarize), the source (the Atlas launch emails this week), the context (a brief for the executive before the Business Operations Review), and the expectations (grouped, five bullets). Every prompt on the Prompts tab is built the same way.

3 A few habits that make prompts better

Be specific about the output.

Ask for a table, a number of bullets, a word count, or a tone. Vague in, vague out.

Give it your content.

Type / to point Copilot at a specific email, meeting, file, or person, so it works from your real information instead of guessing.

Iterate — it's a conversation.

The first answer is a starting point. Follow up with "make it shorter," "more formal," or "add a section on risks." Copilot remembers the thread.

Always review.

Copilot gives you a fast first draft, not the final word. Check names, numbers, and dates before you send or share.

The exception queue is long. You want to know what actually needs attention first.

Excel
Analyze this exception report and group the items by root cause, flag anything open for more than 48 hours, and tell me the three items with the highest financial or client impact that I should escalate first.

2 Document the process, once

A process only lives in one person's head. You want it written down before that's a problem.

Word
Draft a standard operating procedure for [the process], based on the steps I describe: purpose, step-by-step instructions, the systems involved, common exceptions and how to handle them, and who to escalate to if something goes wrong. Use clear numbered steps a new team member could follow.

3 Build the daily operations dashboard summary

Leadership wants the daily numbers in three sentences, not a spreadsheet.

Excel · or Microsoft 365 Copilot
Analyze today's operations metrics workbook and give me a three-sentence summary of volume, turnaround time, and error rate versus yesterday and versus the weekly average, and flag anything that's trending the wrong way.

4 Prep the post-incident review

Something broke. You want a clear, blameless summary ready for the review meeting.

Word
Using the incident timeline and Teams messages from [link the incident channel], draft a post-incident summary: what happened, when it was detected, the impact, the root cause if known, and the corrective actions with owners. Keep the tone factual and blameless.

5 Catch up on the operations channel

You stepped away during a busy shift. You want to know what happened without scrolling through everything.

Teams
Catch me up on what I missed in [this operations channel] over the last shift. Pull out any incidents, escalations, or unresolved issues, and list anything that needs my decision or sign-off.

When to use what

Which Copilot should you reach for?

The same task often works in more than one place. This is a recommendation, not a rule — it points you at the most convenient option and explains why. As you move left to right, you trade speed for depth.

1 The two choices

1 · Copilot Chat

Conversational AI for drafting, Q&A, and ideation. You prompt, it responds, and you drive the conversation — answers come back in seconds. Best for quick drafts, summaries, brainstorming, translating, and looking things up.Where: Copilot chat

2 · In the app

Copilot inside the open document, in editing mode — so it works on your real file, your real thread, your real numbers. Best for drafting in Word, analyzing data in Excel, building slides in PowerPoint, and writing email in Outlook.Where: Word · Excel · PowerPoint · Outlook · Teams

2 Which one for which task

Task or need Copilot Chat In the app Why
Brainstorm ideas and find information ★ Best ✓ Both can. Chat is fastest for open brainstorming and a quick lookup.
Draft an email or summarize a thread ✓ ★ Best Best in Outlook — it works on the real thread with your context. Chat works for something quick and standalone.
Prepare for or synthesize a meeting ✓ ★ Best In the app it uses the real Teams meeting. Use Chat if you point it at the right meeting.
Create or edit one document, deck, or workbook – ★ Best Inside the app, in editing mode, it works with your real file, data, and formulas.

★ Best option    ✓ Also works    – Not recommended

Rule of thumb: Copilot Chat for a quick, standalone answer · the in-app Copilot for one real file, thread, or meeting. If you're not sure, start in Chat — it's the fastest way to find out whether the task needs more than a quick answer.

3 What each one looks like in practice

The same two choices, as prompts you can copy. Notice how the shape of the prompt changes with the tool.

Use Copilot Chat when you need an answer, not an artifact

Microsoft 365 Copilot
Catch me up on [the initiative]. Pull the key updates, decisions, and open risks from my email and Teams over the last week, tell me what's waiting on me, and flag anything that looks like it's slipping. Keep it to eight bullets.
Microsoft 365 Copilot
Brainstorm ten ways we could frame [this change] for our clients. For each one, give me a one-line headline and who it would land best with. Don't write the full message yet — I want to pick a direction first.

Use Copilot in the app when the work is the file

Outlook
Draft a reply to this thread that confirms our position, asks for the two data points we're still missing, and proposes a call on Thursday. Keep it under 150 words and match the tone of the thread.
Excel
Analyze this workbook and tell me which lines moved most versus last quarter. Add a column showing the variance as a percentage, highlight anything that moved more than ten percent, and give me a two-sentence summary of what's driving the change.
PowerPoint
Turn [the attached document] into a six-slide deck: the situation, the three options, the recommendation, the risks, the cost, and next steps. Keep the wording tight enough to read from the back of the room.
